Toolkit mockup

One cabinet, six survivor-centered jobs.

The station is not a public rape kit. It is a clear, beautiful, evidence-aware routing tool: get safe, reach an advocate, find medical and forensic care, preserve choices, and avoid preventable harm while trained responders take over.

Safety boundary

Instructions, not investigation.

The toolkit should never ask bystanders to collect evidence, examine injuries, interrogate a survivor, or decide whether an assault occurred. It should route people toward trained care while preserving privacy and choice.

Readiness protocol

Make the right action the easiest action.

A public-building station should be simple enough for a panicked survivor, a friend, a librarian, a campus RA, a security guard, or an airport worker to use without improvising.

Cabinet contents

  • Sealed response card with plain-language steps.
  • RAINN and local hotline QR cards with short typed URLs.
  • Nearest SAFE/SANE care card with update date.
  • Printed survivor choice bag with inclusive instructions if approved.
  • Staff mini-card for supportive first response.

Staff script

  • "I am sorry this happened. You are not in trouble."
  • "Would you like privacy, a phone, an advocate, or medical care?"
  • "You can choose what happens next."
  • "I will not ask details you do not want to share."

Paper bag guidance

  • Use language that includes every survivor: adults, teens, men, women, LGBTQ+ people, disabled people, immigrants, visitors, and people unsure what happened.
  • Use only for dry clothing or items when local protocol permits.
  • Do not seal wet items in plastic.
  • Do not label with details in public view.
  • Bring items to trained medical or forensic care as soon as possible.

Maintenance

  • Assign an owner for monthly checks.
  • Review local contacts every 90 days.
  • Replace opened cards and paper bags immediately.
  • Audit privacy, accessibility, and language access.
